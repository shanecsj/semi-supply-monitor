01
PROBLEM
Disruption news for RAM and GPU supply arrives fragmented, and mostly irrelevant.
A quake bulletin from USGS, an 8-K from EDGAR, a Federal Register export-control notice, and eight trade outlets each break a different piece of the same week. Almost none of it moves the chain. The few items that do are indistinguishable from the rest until somebody who knows the graph reads them — and that person does not have two hours a day.

02
TARGET USER
Capacity and procurement planners at server OEMs and data-centre buyers.
People who must hold a current mental model of DRAM, HBM and CoWoS supply because their build plan depends on it — and who today assemble that model by hand, from trade press, over coffee, inconsistently.
Unvalidated. We have not yet run a single planner interview — see the riskiest assumption below.
EXPLICITLY NOT FOR
Traders looking for an edge. The market reprices these events inside one session.
Anyone needing shipment-level telemetry. RAM and GPUs move by air; free air-freight telemetry does not exist.
Consumers tracking GPU retail prices. We stop at the channel, not the shelf.

04
RISKIEST ASSUMPTION
That awareness is worth having after the market has already repriced.
Chokepoint claims no speed edge, and it takes no directional view. Both are deliberate — the evidence below is what forced them. But it means the product's entire value rests on a reader who needs to understand the chain rather than trade it. If the only people who care about supply disruption are the ones who wanted to be first, there is no product here.
This is the assumption we are testing next, and it is not testable from a dataset. It needs planner interviews.
HOW WE PLAN TO KILL IT
A
Eight interviews with planners at OEMs and DC buyers. One question decides it: when a fab goes down, what do you actually do in the next week?
B
Ship the digest to five of them weekly for a month. Measure opens and forwards, not stars.
C
Backtest recall: replay twelve months of known disruptions and count how many the filter would have surfaced.

LESSON 1 — NO SPEED EDGE
The whole repricing happened inside one session. On free daily data there is nothing left to be early to, so we stopped trying and built for comprehension instead.
LESSON 2 — THE SIGN IS BACKWARDS
A disruption is bullish for the disrupted chain's sellers. A monitor that scored risk as a magnitude and implied a direction would have been confidently, expensively wrong.

WHAT THIS IS NOT
It is not a trading signal generator, and that is a design decision rather than a missing feature.
NO DIRECTION
We report what happened and how it propagates. The market annotation is attached and left uninterpreted, because the Hualien study showed the naive interpretation is inverted.
NO TELEMETRY THEATRE
Shipping is tracked as news and chokepoint events. RAM and GPUs fly; free air-freight telemetry does not exist, and free ocean data covers the least relevant leg.
NO QUIET DEGRADING
Without an API key the pipeline still runs on a deterministic heuristic — and says so, at confidence 0.35, on every entry it produces.
